I wanted to make another serious post, but I know the world needs anything but more of those. So fine, here's my shitty attempt to arrange everything I have experienced in a top ten list. It is absolutely not exhaustive, and probably reflects my state of mind more than I'd like to admit, but I'll try anyway.

I highly encourage you to also write something like this. Full of things you actually like, and/or things that make you feel *alive*. It might take some time, and it might be difficult, but it is extremely efficient at making your brain reconsider what is *benign* and what is *special*.

---
###  Number 10: A walk at midnight with no objective.
Sometimes, I find myself absolutely pinned by deadlines and work. My executive dysfunction becomes crippling, making it hard for me to move, think or even breathe. When buried to the neck with stuff to do, the only thing that really makes me feel better is wasting time.

And that's what this is - the ideal time waste. You can breathe the crisp cold air, stare at the few stars visible through the blaring street lights, and walk around campus wondering whether you will ever make it. It is the peace of being invisible in a motionless world. This might be the one thing I'll miss about being a University student.

###  Number 9: Waking up after a really nice nap.
You know this feeling. You forgot to set an alarm, and now you've napped for wayyyy too long. Your throat's very dry and your head's buzzing a tiny bit. You can't find your glasses. Which year is it again? 

You know this feeling. It's a mixture of regret and peace. It's a mixture of numbness and urgency. And then, it's over, and it's time to get back to life.

###  Number 8: The feeling when you're finally done with something difficult.
Have you ever done something really, really difficult? Something requiring not tens, but hundreds or thousands of hours of non-stop work? Something that requires your complete body and soul 7 days a week. Once it's done, what do you feel? 

I don't feel triumphant. I don't feel happy. I feel underwhelmed. Empty. Hollow. I ask myself "What now? Is it really over?" Or, maybe, I can't even process that it's over. It can't say whether it feels good or bad, but it does feel incredibly *special*. 

###  Number 7: Being a little mean to your friends!
Look, I can't defend this. I just like being a little mean to my friends! Pulling their leg, making small comments about their hairline, or just poking them and calling them a meanie when they poke back. It is harmless, it gets me closer to them, and it is *incredibly* funny to watch them try to escape the allegations of "being a stinky small baby that smells". But one thing I like more than being mean to my friends is -

###  Number 6: Being (secretly) nice to your friends :3
It is *mind-blowing* to me when people say they don't like their friends. I will take a *bullet* for my friends. I have done, and continue to do, everything in my power to comfort and help my friends during tough periods in their lives. It is not worth associating with a person from whom you have to hide a piece of yourself. I want to see all the pieces of my friends. I want to always be there for my friends.

###  Number 5: Music.
Music melts time together. It sews together the seam between an hour and a minute, the difference between a step and a lap, the contrast between a spot and a stroke. It makes seconds worth remembering and minutes pass by in seconds. Music rocks (Pun intended).

###  Number 4: Being praised by someone you respect.
I don't think my mind can comprehend people being proud of me. When someone I respect says that the feeling is mutual, my mind immediately decides to stop working. Hell, I don't think my mind can understand people *liking* me, although I have gotten used to it, thanks to my wonderful friends. But *compliments*????

###  Number 3: Consumerism.
I'm embarrassed this is so high on the list. I understand the fetishization of commodities, and maybe this makes me a 'bad' leftist, but buying something nice - something you have wanted for a long time - relieves an itch. Maybe I should not be surprised that this is so high on the list, given this is the force keeping the economy afloat, but alas - Understanding propaganda does not make one immune to it.

###  Number 2: When you have nothing on your to-do list.
While some of these are about wasting time, this is more about utilizing time when there is nothing to do. The feeling when you can either spend a week in bed, or pick up an old hobby, or revisit old friends - because it’s all the same. The feeling of indecisiveness at every action, and annoyance at the fact that there is nothing to do, despite all the time you have. I miss when every day was like this.

###  Number 1: Failing, but continuing to try.
Don’t mistake me - It doesn't feel good. Failure hurts because of the very structure of our society and its fear of mortality, and it is anything but easy. But, sometimes, it feels *right* to keep failing. To keep trying. To live another day, and to fail again. Sometimes, it’s not about the goal, and it’s not about the path, but it’s about the fact that you have to keep walking.

And that, I think, is the most special thing I have experienced.
